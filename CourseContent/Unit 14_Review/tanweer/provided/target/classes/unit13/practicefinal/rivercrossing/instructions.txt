A Farmer has a Fox, a Hen, and Seed (collectively known as cargo), that they
need to transport to the other bank of the river by boat. 

- There are two banks: East and West
- The boat can travel from either bank to the other
= On each trip from one bank to the other, the Farmer can travel with only one
  of Fox, Hen, or Seed, or the Farmer can travel alone
- The Farmer will need to make several trips from each bank to the other, alone
  or with a single cargo item
- The Fox and Hen cannot be left together on the bank opposite the Farmer or
  else the Fox will eat the Hen
- The Hen and Seed cannot be left together on the bank opposite the Famer or
  else the Hen will eat the Seed
- The Farmer, Fox, Hen, and Seed, start on the East bank
- The algorithm stops when the Farmer, Fox, Hen, and Seed are on the West bank

It is recommended that you begin by identifying the invalid and goal states from
the description above.
  
Implement a backtracking algorithm to achieve this Goal by completing the
following methods in the RiverCrossing class:
- getSuccessors()
- isValid()
- isGoal()
  
Enumerations are provided for
- River.Bank: EAST or WEST
  - getOpposite(Bank bank) - helper method that returns the opposite bank of the
    one given
- Cargo: FOX, HEN, SEED
  
A River class is also provided and represents which bank the Farmer and each
Cargo item is is located at a given point and provides these methods
- River() - Constructor that initializes the start of the trip to the Farmer and
  Cargo on East bank
- River(River river) - A copy constructor
- getFarmerLocation() - returns the bank that the Farmer is on
- getCargo(Bank bank) - returns a set of Cargo that is on the given bank (may be
  empty)
- crossRiver(cargo) - The Farmer crosses the river to the opposite side with the
  given Cargo item.  If the Farmer is crossing alone, the cargo parameter should
  be null
- toString(), equals(), hashCode()

You should not need more methods to implement a solution and should not alter
the Enumerations or River class.
